RML

Desktop milling machine — machining a test workpiece in the lab

Why it exists

Every machine in this lab needs jigs, fixtures and one-off parts before it can be tested. Rather than waiting on a job shop, the lab mills that work itself — the desktop mill is the benchtop end of the machine family.

How it's built

An anodised aluminium frame on linear guides, ball-screw driven axes, a 24,000 RPM spindle. Designed and machined in-house; the 300 × 200 × 100 mm work envelope is sized for the parts our other projects actually need.

What it's for

Small precision parts, prototypes and tooling — the machine that keeps the other machines moving.

RML

Porous graphite aerostatic spindle — working prototype, IMTEX 2025

How it works

Every precision machine is limited by how accurately its spindle can rotate. This one has nothing to touch: the shaft floats on a film of air only microns thick, metered through a sleeve of porous graphite that lets air seep uniformly over its whole surface. The film is stable at every speed — including zero — so there is no friction, no wear, almost no heat and almost no vibration. The only maintenance is clean, dry air.

Where it is used

Diamond and optical finishing — diamond turning and ultra-precision grinding of glass, ceramics and hard crystals need a spindle without friction, vibration or thermal drift.

Metrology instruments — a measuring machine is only as accurate as its rotation reference; a wear-free, drift-free spindle keeps instruments in calibration for years.

Aerospace and semiconductor precision parts — micro-bores, micro-milling, features held to a fraction of a micron.

Where it stands

Working prototype, designed and built by our industry partner CMTI. CMTI's published Technology Profile documents the platform. The design is shared with us; our part is cost — same accuracy, a fraction of the price. We believe in open source; where collaboration and investment require it, some products stay closed.

RML

Tabletop 4-axis mill — on the shop floor with Max Auto Cables Bengaluru

Why it exists

The desktop mill covers small work; the tabletop covers everything else that fits on a bench — a full four-axis machine in a compact footprint, designed with Max Auto Cables Bengaluru and built in the lab.

How it's built

A heavy machined base, precision linear guideways and ball screws on every axis, driven by a custom LinuxCNC controller. Absolute linear scales hold the axes to ±15 µm.

The fourth axis

A rotary B-axis turns the workpiece while the tool cuts — pockets, curves and compound angles in a single setup, no re-fixturing.
